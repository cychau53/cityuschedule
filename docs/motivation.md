# Motivation

Course registration means choosing more than course titles. Students often compare lectures, tutorials, and other sections while keeping already-registered classes in mind. A schedule that looks plausible as a list can still contain overlapping sessions, awkward breaks, or too little time to get between buildings. Checking every combination manually takes time and makes it easy to overlook a conflict.

`cityuschedule` addresses that planning friction by turning timetable information into a weekly view and a set of practical checks. A student can inspect an AIMS timetable, compare a new course's section bundles against it, or explore course plans under personal constraints. The goal is to support a more informed decision before registration—not to automate or replace the official registration process.

The project favors inputs students can prepare and inspect: a saved AIMS timetable, a copied AIMS offering table, or a CSV of sessions. This makes the basic workflow useful without asking students to hand their credentials to the package. Some catalogues and course lookups may use network access, and all course information should be checked against official sources before acting on it.
